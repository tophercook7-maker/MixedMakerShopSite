## What a Complete Profile Looks Like

A solid Google Business Profile is more than just a name and address. It’s a mini‑website that shows up in search results and Google Maps. Here’s what you should fill out:

- **Name** – Use the exact name people use when they talk about your business. No extra words or abbreviations.
- **Address** – A street address that matches what’s on your sign and on any other online listings. If you’re a service‑only business, use the “service area” field.
- **Phone number** – A local number that rings in Hot Springs or the surrounding area. Make sure it’s the same number on your website.
- **Hours** – Accurate opening hours for every day, including holidays. Update them when you close early or open late.
- **Photos** – At least five high‑quality images: your storefront, staff, products, and customers in action. Google likes fresh photos.
- **Description** – A short paragraph (50‑100 words) that tells people what you do and why you’re different. Keep it local and honest.
- **Categories** – Pick the primary category that best describes your business, then add secondary ones. This helps Google match you to relevant searches.
- **Attributes** – Add any relevant attributes such as “wheelchair accessible,” “free Wi‑Fi,” or “pet friendly.”
- **Services** – List the main services or products you offer. Each service can have a brief description and a price (optional).
- **Q&A** – Pre‑populate common questions. This gives you a chance to highlight your strengths before someone asks.
- **Posts** – Use the posting feature to share updates, promotions, or events. Posts show up on your profile and can drive clicks.

When every field is filled out, Google has enough data to rank you in local search and show prospects a clear picture of what you offer.

## Common Mistakes I See

1. **Misspelled names or addresses** – Even a single typo can split your reviews or make Google think you’re a different business.
2. **Missing photos** – Profiles without photos rank lower and look untrustworthy.
3. **Incomplete hours** – If your hours are wrong, people will call you at the wrong time and leave a bad review.
4. **Not verifying the listing** – Unverified profiles can be edited by anyone, which opens the door to misinformation.
5. **Wrong categories** – Choosing a category that doesn’t match your business can hide you from people searching for what you actually provide.
6. **Ignoring the Q&A** – People often ask questions before they call. If you don’t answer them, you lose the chance to convert interest into a call.
7. **Not responding to reviews** – Reviews are a conversation. If you don’t reply, you’re missing out on building trust.

These are simple errors that can cost you calls and money. Fixing them is quick, but the payoff is huge.

## Why Reviews Matter More Than Your Website

When a potential customer looks up a plumber in Hot Springs, the first thing they’ll see is your Google Business Profile. If it’s clean, up‑to‑date, and filled with positive reviews, they’ll feel confident calling you. Your website can be beautiful, but it’s often a secondary factor in local searches.

- **Social proof** – A handful of five‑star reviews tells people you’re reliable. A website can’t convey that instant trust.
- **Search ranking** – Google uses reviews as a ranking signal. More reviews and higher ratings push you higher in local results.
- **Information at a glance** – A review often includes a photo of the work done, a brief description, and the price. That’s more useful than a long page on your site.
- **Customer engagement** – Responding to a review shows you care. That interaction can turn a curious browser into a loyal client.

In short, your profile is the first impression. If it’s polished, people will call you. If it’s messy, they’ll look elsewhere.

## How I Build a Profile

1. **Claim the listing** – Search for your business name and click “Claim this business.” If you’re not the owner, you’ll need to verify ownership.
2. **Verify** – Google usually sends a postcard with a code. Enter it to confirm you’re the real owner. If you prefer, you can verify by phone if the option is available.
3. **Fill out the details** – Use the checklist above. Don’t skip anything. The more complete, the better.
4. **Add photos** – Take a few good shots with your phone or a camera. I like to show the front of the shop, a worker in action, and a happy customer if possible.
5. **Set up Q&A** – Anticipate the top three questions people ask and answer them. For example, “Do you offer same‑day service?”
